# The Dry Stair — treatment

Silkscreen Travel Poster. 40.0 s, 1920×1080, 24 fps, English, Kokoro `af_heart`. One full-bleed print. Fictional place: Brine Stair, a tide stair that exists only as unprinted paper. No real coast, park, agency, or poster is portrayed.

## 1. Three structures

1. **Trail list.** Three walks, three stat rows, a wall of posters. The style’s obvious guide. Rejected: it is the cliché, and the hour never becomes a problem.
2. **Palette re-ink.** The same harbor pulled at dawn, noon, and dusk. Pretty, but time passing is only a colour swap. The path does not change its mind.
3. **Misprint.** One sheet. The stair is a knockout of bare paper. The first time-pull prints 3:40 off register, the layers drift apart, the music stops, and one clamp snaps 4:10 over the mistake. Chosen. The native move (slip, then snap) is the plot. The ending echoes the opening: the accent hour is the only colour that was true, and the stair was paper the whole time.

Opening image: a red sun, alone, pulled across cream stock through the mesh. Ending: the same sheet clamped in a ticket window, the last line sitting on the wood beneath it. Score: nylon guitar, pizzicato bass, brushes, a harmonica reed. It thins, stops, and returns as the opening figure.

## 2. Logline and arc

A printer keeps missing the hour of a tide stair; the path is the paper the water never covers, and the last pull snaps 4:10 into register.

- **Setup.** The sun, the stepped sky, the ridge, the water, and the cliff print in order. The stair is the hole they leave.
- **Turn.** The hour prints as 3:40 and the layers slip. Silence. A clamp. 4:10 covers the mistake and the planes snap shut.
- **Ending.** The band prints the facts. The camera passes a pine and pulls back to the ticket window. The sheet is the only stair left.

## 3. Benchmark

- **WPA screenprints and Fifty-Nine Parks** (grammar only). Learn: four to six opaque inks, stepped bands instead of a sky gradient, a dark information band, paper as the lightest colour, type drawn into the picture.
- **Do not take:** any real park name, agency, lettering, logo, layout, or melody. No halftone, no fluorescent overprint, no key-block line.

## 6. Cue map

Tempo stays 96. Acceleration is subdivision, not a tempo change: after the snap, stair-dashes and guitar notes land on eighths (0.3125 s). Elsewhere, strums land on the pull.

- **0.00–5.00** Guitar only, then a bass note when the sun lands (1.875) and when the sky lands (5.00). Brushes from bar 2.
- **5.00–15.55** Strum plus bass on each layer landing (5.00, 7.50, 10.00, 12.50). Brushes on the backbeat. Harmonica silent.
- **15.55–17.50** Music out.
- **17.50** Downbeat: guitar chord, bass, harmonica, clamp.
- **17.81–21.25** Eighth-note nylon plucks with the dashes.
- **22.50–31.25** Wider strums again while the band is read. One harmonica note at 25.00.
- **32.15–33.25** Music out.
- **33.25** Slide A3→D4. Opening strum returns at 35.00 and 37.50.

## 8. Type and subtitles

Cormorant Garamond, condensed by a horizontal scale (the face on hand is not a condensed gothic). Sky name tracked out in the near ink. Band: accent for 01 and 4:10, paper-coloured type for the name and the labels, a paper rule. Subtitles are a near-ink strip along the bottom of the frame. Each line is a band pull that covers the line before it. The strip is not a floating caption. Spoken “Four ten” is subtitled “4:10”. The strip stays out of `TEXTS`; the poster words are what the reading check times.

Palette, six inks plus the stock: paper `#F6F1E4`, sky `#F6CD98`, stripe `#EE9A73`, far `#9A7FA8`, mid `#3E7079`, near `#1E3B3F`, accent `#E8553D`.
