# NIGHT ELEVATOR — treatment

Art Deco · 16 s · 1920×1080 · 24 fps · stereo.
A real style demo for FilmCode, made entirely in code.

## The brief

The library has a teaser for Art Deco ("The Type Specimen") but no real demo. Make
a short film that lives in the style's own grammar — warm lacquer black, engraved
gold line, airbrushed geometry, one vertical axis — and that is a story rather than
a style card. No narration: the type, the dial and the bulbs carry the plot.

## Three candidate structures

1. **The exhibition.** Five pavilions of a world's fair, one idea behind each
   door. Strong deco subject, but five thresholds in sixteen seconds is a list,
   not a film.
2. **The telegram.** Ticker tape types the premise; a gold route crosses the
   continent. Beautiful, but it makes the medium typographic and the ending is
   only a punchline.
3. **The night elevator.** The last lift of the night climbs forty floors before
   midnight. The floor dial is the countdown, the doors are the thresholds, the
   roof sign is the payoff. **Chosen:** one object, one goal, one turn, and every
   one of the style's native moves (doors, dial, counted light, cutaway) is part
   of the story instead of decoration.

## Logline and arc

One elevator wants the roof before midnight. **Setup:** the tower draws itself in
gold and a title card unfolds. **Turn:** the climb accelerates, the dial counts,
the clock runs to 11:59, the power dips. **Ending:** at 12:00 the doors open, the
roof sign lights one letter at a time, and the shot flattens into a printed
poster with one bulb still burning.

## Benchmark

- **Learn:** Cassandre's poster scale and steep centred perspective; the *Great
  Gatsby* (2013) title sequence's gold line drawing itself on black; Busby
  Berkeley's counted, symmetrical reveals.
- **Do not take:** any poster layout, any real building, the film's typeface
  designs, or the demo teaser's "Type Specimen" subject.

## Cue map

120 BPM, 4/4, eight bars. Stride piano and pizzicato bass from S2; brushes enter
at the climb; the music drops out at the power dip; a single chime and a warm
final chord carry the roof and the poster. Bulb ignitions are on the beat grid;
each floor tick is a relay click. No generic piano-and-strings pad.

## Sound design

| Section | Ambience | Foley | Music |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | faint room tone | line engrave, sheen shimmer | low piano boom, bell |
| S2 | lobby air | glove, button click, door slide, latch | stride piano, pizz bass |
| S3 | motor hum rising | floor relays, arrival settle, power dip | brushes, walking bass, piano |
| S4 | roof wind | door slide, bulb ignitions, clock chime | airy chord, bell |
| S5 | none | one last bulb ping | warm final chord |

## Type

Limelight for the title (architecture: a stepped gold bar); Poiret One for the
dial, clock and small caps; Josefin Sans for the explainer line. Titling and the
lamp sign are one system: both unfold from the centre.
