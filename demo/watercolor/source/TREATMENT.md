# Alborada — treatment

**Style:** Watercolor Brush (`styles/watercolor/STYLE.md`) · **Length:** 45.0 s · **1920×1080 @ 30 fps** · **English**, warm female narrator
**Topic:** one harvest morning on a family coffee farm. The farm is an invented stand-in ("Finca Alborada", 1,720 m) — no real place, family or brand is portrayed, and no number in the film is claimed as sourced data.

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A. One locked page, a morning painted in.** Blank paper; each beat paints the next thing on the same sheet while a hand-written clock note advances (4:50 → 6:10 → 9:00). Strength: purest expression of "the painting is the film". Weakness: static camera for 45 s, and the farm never reads as a place with scale.

**B. A continuous handscroll across the farm.** One long lateral dolly from the forest edge downhill through the rows, the mill, the drying patio and into the kitchen. Objects paint themselves in just ahead of the camera; the light on the paper changes from blue dawn to gold. Strength: the style's own camera grammar (a painted world read across space), one unbroken move, a natural build. Weakness: little room for a turn unless something interrupts the scroll.

**C. Anatomy of one cherry.** A botanical plate: the cherry, then the bean inside, the mill, the roast, the cup — each part a labelled close-up. Strength: precise, journal-like, numbers land. Weakness: it is a process diagram, not a morning; the family disappears into a specimen.

**Chosen: B, with C as its opening.** The film begins as a specimen study (one cherry, labelled, painting itself) and then the camera pulls back out of the plate into the place the cherry came from — the world keeps expanding until it contracts to a cup. The turn is a real event in the morning (the mill, and the one silence before it), not a cut in a diagram. This also keeps the film far from the demo's specimen-plate structure: the plate is only the first 3 seconds, then it becomes a place.

## 2. Logline and arc

> A daughter walks one harvest morning down her family's hillside — picking, pulping, raking — and ends where every morning ends: one cup, poured for the people who did the work.

- **Setup (0–9 s):** macro on wet pigment: a red cherry paints itself on blank paper. Pull back — it is one cherry on a branch; pull back again — a row on a hillside at first light.
- **Turn (21–31 s):** the scroll arrives at the wet mill. The day's picking is poured into a machine and becomes water and skins. At 29.4 s the brush lifts: music out, paper and room tone only. The morning has spent something.
- **Ending (39–45 s):** the drying patio, then the kitchen table: one cup, steam painted as a blooming wash. The camera pulls back until the whole film is revealed as one page, with a pencil note in the corner — *picked before six*. The first image (the red cherry) returns as the accent on the cup's rim.

## 3. Benchmark

**Learn from:** Frédéric Back's *The Man Who Planted Trees* — light as the clock, patience with a painted world, sound carried by wind and work rather than score; naturalist field-journal plates (specimen + hand-written note + a measured value) for the annotation layer; long handscroll painting for the idea that a place is read by moving across it, not by cutting it.
**Do not take:** Back's characters, drawings or palette; any real botanical plate, farm, brand or logo; any existing melody. No real farm's name or history.

## 4. Shot list

Every shot is a camera move inside one continuous painted world; no hard cuts, only brush-edge wipes and mist.

| # | t (s) | Framing | Move | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.0–3.0 | Extreme close-up (one cherry fills 40 % of frame height) | Locked, 2 % push | Hook: paint itself is the subject; the wet bloom is the first motion. |
| 2 | 3.0–9.0 | Close → wide | Pull-back in three stages (cherry → branch → row → hillside), then a small upward tilt to the dawn sky | Reveals scale in one unbroken move; the specimen becomes a place. |
| 3 | 9.0–21.0 | Wide, then full as hands enter | Long lateral dolly right, ~90 px/s, parallax layers | The journey down the slope; the family at work; time passes as light. |
| 4 | 21.0–30.0 | Medium → close | Dolly-in and tilt down the steps into the mill; a small push on the hopper | Arrival, and the turn: the picking is consumed. The tilt is the only vertical move. |
| 5 | 30.0–39.0 | Wide high angle | Locked during the burst, then a slow crane down to hand level | The burst needs a fixed frame to be read; the drop re-enters the human scale. |
| 6 | 39.0–45.0 | Medium → wide → whole sheet | Push to the cup, then a long pull-back to the full page | Intimacy, then the film admits it was a page; the ending echoes shot 1. |

Signature shot: **shot 3** — one 12-second lateral dolly in which the palette itself moves from blue dawn to warm gold across the frame (palette as data), and every object is painted by the camera's arrival.

## 6. Cue map

- 80 BPM, 4/4; bars at 0, 3, 6 … 45. Every cut-free section change lands on a bar line; the burst lands on beat 1 of bar 11 (30.9 s is its paint-in peak, 1.5 beats after the bar).
- **Instruments:** felt piano (lead), fingerpicked acoustic guitar (B), clarinet/flute (D), glockenspiel (A, E), kalimba (B), warm string pad (B, D), soft bass (D). No drums.
- **Process:** A one note per bar, wide spacing → B a two-bar guitar figure that repeats and gains a counter-line → C subtractive (parts removed until piano alone) → silence → D additive from the bottom up, all parts arrive by 36.0 → E solo piano, one unresolved chord that resolves on the pencil note at 44.0.
- Palette-as-data is mirrored in the score: the drone's filter opens across shot 3 (9–21 s) with the light.

Three real silences: 29.4–30.0 (the big one), 21.0 (a beat of air before the water J-cut lands), 42.5 (brush lifted before the final note).

## 8. Type and subtitles

- **Titles:** "Alborada" in Cormorant Garamond, ink at 80 %, small (48 px) in the upper-left margin, with "a morning on the farm" beneath in Caveat.
- **Subtitles:** serif italic, ~36 px, ink at 78 %, paper halo, bottom-centre, max two balanced lines; never over the hands or the cup.
- **Hand notes:** Caveat, drawn twice for texture, revealed left-to-right, each with a pen leader line; mono (IBM Plex Mono, wide tracking) for measured values only: `1,720 m`, `04:50`, `40 %`.
- Numbers are written as digits on screen and spelled out in the TTS text.
