# One Rectangle — treatment

**Style:** Ukiyo-e (`styles/ukiyoe/STYLE.md`) · **Length:** 40.0 s · **1920×1080 @ 24 fps** · **English**, Kokoro `af_heart`
**Topic:** an invented salt shore. One raker, one rectangle of ground, one tide. No real place, person, brand or famous print is portrayed.

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A. Stations of a ferry.** Five sheets slide right to left: pier, channel, mid-river, far bank, the empty boat. Strength: the handscroll is native. Weakness: it is the road-journey cliché, and the boat never wants anything.

**B. Alternate impressions of one rectangle.** The same key block — a raked rectangle on a shore, a small figure, one pine — is printed twice. Morning colours land and she finishes the rake. The tide is unprinted paper that floods past the border and washes the blocks away. Dusk colours print onto the same lines, she rakes again, and this impression holds. Strength: one subject, one turn, and the medium itself does the erasure. Weakness: only two views, so the series idea has to live in placement (where the rectangle sits) rather than in many sheets.

**C. The album of three generations.** A triptych: grandmother, mother, child, each a sheet, joining at the end. Strength: the join is a native move. Weakness: three lives cannot be read in forty seconds, and the tide would be decoration.

**Chosen: B.** The constant subject is the rectangle, not a traveller. Opening is an extreme crop of the rake's teeth in the salt (the ground is already the image). The signature is the tide leaving the sheet: water crosses both the sheet clip and the ink border onto the mount, then the colour blocks wash back to paper while the salt ridges stay white. Ending bookends the opening: the same teeth rest across a rectangle that this time remains printed, and a seal stamps the day's mark. The scroll never travels to a second place.

## 2. Logline and arc

> A salt-raker finishes one rectangle of shore at dawn; the tide erases the print back to paper; she rakes the same ground at dusk, and the evening keeps it.

- **Setup (0–12 s):** rake teeth fill the sheet, then the camera pulls out to the morning impression. She draws the last strokes and stops, as if the work were done.
- **Turn (12–24 s):** a held breath, then the tide. Water breaks the border. Colour washes off. A blank sheet remains, salt still white because it was never ink.
- **Ending (24–40 s):** the same key block takes dusk pigments, block by block. She rakes again, slower, then stops. The push finds the rectangle still there. The seal stamps. The first image — the rake — rests in the frame instead of working.

## 3. Benchmark

**Learn:** Hiroshige's habit of a small figure under a huge sky, and a foreground post or branch that crops the view; the way a surimono uses one red sparingly; kabuki's *ma* — clappers, then silence, then one sound that matters. Grammar only.
**Do not take:** *The Great Wave*, *Red Fuji*, *Sudden Shower*, any station of the Tōkaidō, any named actor or courtesan, any existing melody. No Hokusai crest, no copied wave claw as the subject. The water here is a flat banded tide, not a famous wave.

## 6. Cue map

- 60 BPM, 4/4. Bars at 0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 40.
- Tempo does not jump. Pace changes by density: notes thin out, then one glissando, then silence, then the reprint, then a tighter rake that slows.
- **Grid:** shamisen on beat 1 of each bar from 0 to 12, silent 12–22.4, one note at 22.4, then beat 1 again from 24 to 36. Seal on beat 1 of bar 10 (36.0). Push starts on bar 9 (32.0). Tide picture on bar 4 (14.0); the water sound is early, on the upbeat (13.4).
- **Modes:** morning in ritsu on D (D E G A B). From the tide, miyako-bushi on D (D Eb G A Bb). The meri at the end slides A down toward Ab, then releases to G.
- **Instruments:** shamisen (sawari, pulse), shakuhachi (breathy, slides into the note), koto (one descending glissando at 16.0), hyōshigi at the seal. No piano, no pad, no taiko roll — the tide is water, not a drum.
- **Shape:** sparse → one held tone → silence → glissando → silence with a single ringing note → reprint (baren is the rhythm) → last phrase that drops and stops. The score ends with the print still on screen.

## 8. Type

- **Subtitles** are horizontal cartouches: cream slip, thick-and-thin ink border, a small red seal at the left, sumi type centred. They sit in the bottom band (about 150 px) and print in with a short diagonal wipe. The ground line and the figure stay above them.
- **View cartouches** are vertical cream slips on the print: a pale tab "Shore Sheets", the view title below, a seal beside. "Morning Salt" from 6.0, fading as the tide washes. "The Evening Kept" from 36.2 through the hold.
- **Face:** Cormorant Garamond (OFL), copied from `demo/impasto/source/fonts`, weight 500 for the slips and italic for the view title. Titles live inside cartouches, never as floating type.
- No end card, no sign-off, no maker's name.

## 9. Pigment

One set, printed, not blended: paper `#f3e6c8`; sumi `#1c1915`; Prussian blue `#1f4e79`; pale blue `#7ea0b8`; ochre `#c4a15a`; grey-green `#6e7d62`; indigo cloth `#24344a`; beni `#9c2b2b` used only for the sash, the low sun, and the seals. Salt, foam and the blank sky at the top of the bokashi are unprinted paper. No shading on the figure. Far pine is pale and low contrast; the rake in front is dark and saturated.

## 10. What this film refuses

It is not a journey, not a wave, not a mountain, not a rain station, not a triptych, and not a print left unfinished. The second impression is finished, and it stays.
