# Heat Leaves — treatment

**Style:** Chalkboard (`styles/chalkboard/STYLE.md`) · **Length:** 39.0 s · **1920×1080 @ 24 fps** · **English**, calm teacher (Kokoro `af_heart`)
**Topic:** a one-idea classroom correction. People say a cold room means cold came in through the glass. The board writes that mistake, wipes it in one pass, and writes what is true: heat is what moves, and it leaves. Invented lesson — no textbook, brand, or real classroom is portrayed.

The locked delivery is 24 fps and about −14 LUFS. The style sheet's own demo note prefers 30 fps and a −16 mix; this film follows the locked brief, and the mux masters to −14.

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A. One sum, three wrong answers.** A worked problem stays at the top. Three pupil answers are written and wiped one after another, then the right figure. Strength: the wipe is the grammar. Weakness: three wipes become a gag reel, and the board never has to mean one thing.

**B. A word taken apart.** Big letters, one stroke circled in accent chalk, a sliding panel with the older form. Strength: closest to the style's character engine. Weakness: it is the obvious chalkboard film, and this lesson is in English, not a character study.

**C. Write the mistake, wipe it, rewrite it in the same place.** One window drawn in white. A second, paler hand writes "COMES IN" and an arrow pointing inward. One felt pass clears that reading. The same spot becomes "HEAT" / "LEAVES", and the arrow — the only accent — turns outward and runs across the seam to the other panel. Strength: one claim, one correction, the camera barely moves until the line itself pulls it. Weakness: the science has to be said in a few words or it becomes a lecture.

**Chosen: C.** The tracked component is the direction of the heat, not a letter. The opening is a damp, just-wiped slate (a drying streak, then the first word). The ending is the whole wall — frame, tray, clock — with both panels still showing the corrected lesson. The score is sparse nylon guitar, marimba, and pizzicato bass, and it goes quiet for the wipe and for the verdict.

## 2. Logline and arc

> A teacher lets the class write the usual mistake — that cold comes in through the window — then wipes it, and the chalk shows that heat is what travels, and it leaves.

- **Setup (0–11 s):** close on white chalk writing COLD. Pull back: a window is already on the slate, and a pupil's yellow chalk adds COMES IN with an arrow pointing in.
- **Turn (11–20 s):** one wipe. A held breath. HEAT and LEAVES are written in the same places, and the arrow is redrawn outward in orange.
- **Ending (21–39 s):** that orange line crosses the seam; a whip follows it onto the other panel, where the same heat leaves a second window. The camera pulls out until the room is a classroom again.

## 3. Benchmark

**Learn from:** a primary literacy lesson on a sliding double board — one claim, a wrong line, one wipe, the right line in the same place; the camera flat to the slate; chalk speed that still looks like a hand.
**Do not take:** any real teacher's handwriting, any character-etymology lesson, any existing textbook diagram, any melody.

- 80 BPM, 4/4. Bars at 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30, 33, 36, 39.

- **Instruments:** nylon guitar (lead, sparse), marimba (answers), upright pizzicato bass (bar downbeats only). No piano, no pad, no drums.
- **Process:** A one guitar note per bar → B marimba joins, still thin → silence through the wipe and the verdict sentence → C the guitar note is born from the first stroke of LEAVES, bass returns → D a little more marimba on the right panel → E near-silence under the last sentence, then one harmonic over the wide hold.

## 8. Type and subtitles

- **On the board:** keywords only, drawn as chalk centre-lines (not type). White for the claim, yellow for the pupil's wrong line, orange only for the heat's direction. No second language line — the film is English throughout.
- **Sizes:** COLD / HEAT at about 400 world units tall. COMES IN / LEAVES / OUT smaller, with heavier chalk so the stroke stays thick on screen.
- **Subtitles:** one English line, Cormorant Garamond italic, on a soft dark plate above the bottom safe area. Hard in and out with the voice (nothing fades). Each cue held at least 1.8 s and at least the spoken length plus 0.6 s. The plate never covers the words being read.
- **Voice:** short spoken sentences, said while the matching chalk is on the board. Pauses between lines about 0.3 s; the long hold is the silence after the wipe, before "Cold does not travel."
