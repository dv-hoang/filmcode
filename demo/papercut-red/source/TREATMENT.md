# Eighth Fold — treatment

Red Paper-cut. Original story. English. Kokoro `af_heart`. No storyboard. 40.0 s, 1920×1080, 24 fps. Picture locks to this sheet.

## 1. Three structures

1. **Twelve cuts, one year.** A wheel of months, one wedge each. Too much catalogue for forty seconds, and the feeling stays decorative.
2. **Assembly line.** A lantern built from cut parts. Clear craft, thin want: nothing is trying to get somewhere.
3. **An accordion letter that is a channel.** One red sheet, eight layers, one crescent. Unfolded, the holes are the only deep water. The scrap that fell out is the boat. Chosen: the cut is the cause of the ending, the want is simple (get across), and the picture can be read at thumbnail size — a red bar, eight moons, a small boat.

Opening image: the eighth fold already in the air, past vertical, slapping down onto an indigo table. No title first.
Ending: the lamps of the room go, except the foil lamp behind the sheet. The holes project a path. The boat crosses it. Cut letters: EIGHTH FOLD.
Score shape: sparse pipa, a silence, one gong on the first snap, a rising zheng note per window, a breath, then a slowing flute. Not a celebration tutti.

## 2. Logline and arc

**Logline.** A keeper’s last fold takes one red sheet to eight layers; one crescent cut, and the holes are a channel the scrap itself can cross, once she holds the sheet to a lamp.

- **Setup.** Night table, indigo paper. She finishes the eighth fold and cuts one crescent. The offcut falls.
- **Turn.** After a silence, the sheet snaps open left to right. Eight holes. The offcut is a boat.
- **Ending.** She lifts the sheet to a gold-foil lamp. Only that light remains. The boat crosses the projected holes.

One subject (the folded sheet), one goal (a way across), one turn (the unfold). Native move at the peak: fold–cut–unfold, and the holes are the way home. The scrap’s future is the boat, not noise. Gold foil appears once, as the lamp.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Learn.** Tabletop craft timing: tool, hands, one irreversible cut, then a hold so the result can be read. Chamber pentatonic writing that gets thinner before the snap, so the snap is the loudest paper in the film. Profile acting, layers of paper, no perspective.
- **Do not take.** Festival monsters, a rosette that flies apart into fireworks, a dawn peel, a last pull-back that says the film was a window flower, any named folk character, any existing melody.

Checked against the library demo after this treatment: structure, opening, signature shot, camera path, score shape and ending all differ.

Voice is compressed, then set about 10 dB over the music. Music ducks about 9 dB under the voice, foley about 3 dB. Impacts stay out of the spoken windows. Mix target −14 LUFS. No film grain in the mux.

## 8. Type

- Subtitles: a paper banner, red `#6e0e18` (darker than the sheet), cream `#f6ecd4`, Cormorant Garamond 600 at 44 px, serrated edges, swallow tails, low on the frame. It pastes in on two 12 fps steps. It does not sit on the keeper’s face or the holes.
- Title: yang-cut letters EIGHTH FOLD, red paper, counters left as real holes so the lamp shows through. Letter by letter on 12 fps steps from 34.90. The finished word holds from 35.60 to 40.00 (4.4 s). Sawtooth strips above and below. A small seal (a plum of crescents, cut in code) pops once, after the last letter.
- No sign-off card.

## 9. Picture rules for this film

- China red `#d2201f` for the sheet and the keeper. Near arm and near leg `#e23a32`. Far limbs `#a31a22`. Fold backs `#e4584c`. Stack edges step darker.
- Ground family is indigo only: sky `#243656`, far bank `#31456f`, water `#1b2948`, table `#162033`. Luma stays high enough that a frame is not black.
- Gold `#d4a24a` and a foil chip `#f0d48a` only on the lamp, from 29.40.
- Grain inside each piece (soft-light), then holes punched so they stay holes. Inset slit on figures and flat panels. Hand-cut wobble. 1 px light rim upper-left, dark rim lower-right.
- Faces swap: determined, surprise, smile. One profile eye: two crescents and a pupil hole, never a ring.
- Scissors are black paper cut into blades, rivet behind the tip, tip pointing with the cut.
